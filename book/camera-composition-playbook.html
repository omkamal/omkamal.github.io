Why it matters

Most photos aren’t bad. They’re just unframed.

The camera records everything in front of it. Composition is the choice of what stays in the frame, and where. You can learn it one rule at a time.

Start with the problem you see, then jump to the rules that fix it.

Flat

Nothing pulls the eye in. Depth and layers add the third dimension.

Cluttered

Too many things compete. One clear subject and a simple background calm it down.

Boring

Everything sits in the middle. Placement and lines give the eye a path.

Off-balance

It feels tipped or heavy on one side. Visual weight puts it right.

An honest note about the pictures

The pictures in the book are AI-generated photorealistic images, not photographs, and the book says so. The text was written with AI assistance and edited by the author. The pictures make each rule easy to see. They do not prove that a rule works on a real camera, which is what the exercises are for.

What is in, and what is not

  • In: composition rules for any camera or phone, picture pairs, exercises.
  • Not in: camera settings, gear lists or editing tutorials.
  • Format: a 6 × 9 in PDF for screens, not a printed book.
Good to know

Questions, answered plainly.

Who is this for?

Anyone who takes photos with a phone or a camera and wants them to look more deliberate. You do not need to know photography terms. The glossary explains them.

Do I need a particular camera?

No. The rules are about the frame, not the gear, so a phone is enough. The book has no camera settings or gear lists.

Are the pictures real photographs?

No. They are AI-generated photorealistic images, and the book says so. They make each rule easy to compare. The exercises ask you to try each rule with your own camera.

What format is it?

A PDF in English: 325 pages, 6 × 9 inches, in colour, about 23 MB, with linked contents and bookmarks. It is delivered as a download from Payhip.
